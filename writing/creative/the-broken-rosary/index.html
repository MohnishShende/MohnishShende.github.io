The Broken Rosary

After Kabir and Sultan Bahu

Words have now come to an end.
Language has fulfilled its purpose.
Love itself has become the message,
And beyond love there is no message.

Everyone longs to behold
The One who is the source of all beauty and meaning.
I have fallen in love
Even with not attaining Him.
But this is no ordinary understanding.

People cry out “Lord, Lord”
A hundred thousand times,
Yet the Lord remains beyond their reach.
We keep playing with words,
While Reality escapes our grasp.

Repeat the word “fire”
As long as you wish,
Your lips will never burn.
Place a single spark upon them,
And they ignite at once.

To mistake the word “water”
For water itself is ignorance.
One may chant “water” forever,
Yet still die of thirst.

Those who cling only to the Name
Lose the One who bears the Name.
The fool does no inner work,
He merely comforts himself with words.

The wooden rosary asks,
“Why do you keep turning me?”
Turn instead the beads within your own mind,
And you will find the Beloved at once.

Blessed that my water pot broke.
I am free from carrying water.
The burden has been lifted
From my head.

Watching the grinding mill,
Kabir began to weep.
Between its two grinding stones,
Nothing remains whole.

Everyone notices the grinding stones,
But none notice the still axle.
Whoever remains fixed to that center
Cannot be harmed.

Fasten yourself
To the peg of the Divine Name.
Remain aware
Of every breath that rises and falls,
For this opportunity will not return.

Kabir says:
There is only one well,
Though many draw water from it.
The vessels are different,
But the water within them is one.

My Master planted
The jasmine of “Alif Allah”
Within my heart.
It was nourished
By the waters of Divine Truth.
Its fragrance spread through every vein.
My whole being blossomed like flowers.
Only through a perfect guide
Does this flower bloom within the heart.

Blessed that my rosary broke.
I am free
Even from mechanical worship.
The burden has been lifted
From my head.

Whom shall I worship now?
My rosary has broken.
Congratulate me,
My rosary has broken.
Blessed indeed
That my rosary has broken.

If the drop
Had never been separated from the ocean,
What would have been different?
Nothing except what already is.

The world is deceived
By the illusion of separation.
If there were any God
Other than You,
What difference could it possibly make?

The clay says to the potter,
“Why do you trample me today?
A day will come
When I shall trample you.”

For all things return to dust.

Provenance note

This work is presented as a contemporary English reworking, not as a wholly original composition or a direct translation. It draws on traditional verses and motifs associated with Kabir, including the inward rosary, grinding mill, shared well, and clay speaking to the potter, and on Sultan Bahu’s “Alif Allah Chambey Di Booti.” The arrangement, connective passages, and English phrasing are Mohnish Shende’s. The historical source traditions are public domain; no modern translation is reproduced or claimed as a licensed source.
