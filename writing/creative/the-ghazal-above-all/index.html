The Ghazal Above All

Mohnish Shende

The mehfil had found its rhythm.

I had already sung three ghazals, introducing each with a sher of its own. The audience listened with warmth. Every now and then someone called out Wah! A few others whistled in appreciation.

I smiled.

Now it was time for Momin.

Roya karenge aap bhi pehron isi tarah…

Before I could begin, another whistle rang through the hall.

Someone in the audience turned around. “Arre, bas kijiye!”

Another voice replied, “Unhe pasand aa rahi hai, toh karne dijiye!”

Within moments, the hall was no longer waiting for the ghazal. It was listening to itself.

I stepped towards the microphone. “Arre… arre… dosto.”

The voices faded.

“Aap sab ki mohabbat sar aankhon par.” A few faces softened. “Lekin ek chhoti si guzarish hai.”

The hall grew quiet.

“Ghazal bahut naazuk cheez hoti hai.”

I paused. “Sher ko uski khamoshi de dijiye.”

No one spoke.

“Wah kahiye… Irshad kahiye… jitni dafa dil chahe kahiye.” A gentle ripple of laughter passed through the audience. “Magar sher mukammal ho jaaye… uske baad.”

Another pause. “Aakhir…” I let my eyes travel across the room. “Na yeh shaam meri hai… na aapki. Yeh ghazal ki shaam hai.”

Silence settled over the hall. Not the silence of discomfort. The silence of a mehfil remembering why it had gathered.

I returned to the microphone. “Momin…”

Without another word, I began.

Roya karenge aap bhi pehron isi tarah…

This time, no one interrupted. Only when the matla had ended did the hall answer as one.

“Wah.”

And for the rest of the evening, the mehfil belonged to the ghazal.

Attribution: The quoted opening line is from “Roya Karenge Aap Bhi” by Momin Khan Momin (1800–1852). No modern translation or recorded performance is reproduced.
