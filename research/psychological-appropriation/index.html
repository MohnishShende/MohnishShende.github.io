Psychological Appropriation: A Dimensional Model of Surrogate-Moral Violence

Mohnish Shende · Journal of Forensic Psychology Research and Practice · 2025

Canonical record ↗DOI 10.1080/24732850.2025.2599908

Abstract

Psychological Appropriation describes a surrogate-driven form of moral violence in which offenders believe they are acting empathically, protectively, or redemptively on behalf of others. It reframes moralized violence as a distortion of empathy rather than its absence. The paper situates the construct within psychotic personality organization and develops five interacting domains: Surrogate Identification, Moralized Logic, Symbolic Consent, Affective Idealization, and Narrative Ritualization. Applied to the cases of Adam Lanza and Charles Cullen, the model shows how surrogate morality can transform care into compulsion and offers a unified structure for understanding symbolic and surrogate-moral violence.

Contribution

Introduces Psychological Appropriation as a framework for examining surrogate identification, moralized logic, symbolic consent, affective idealization, and narrative ritualization.

Method

Conceptual model development with case-based illustration.

Limits and availability

The model is theoretical and requires empirical validation. Full-text redistribution rights have not been established, so this site links to the canonical publisher record.
