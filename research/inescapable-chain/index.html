The Inescapable Chain: Human Nature, Exploitation, and the Illusion of Freedom

Mohnish Shende · SSRN · 2025

Canonical record ↗DOI 10.2139/ssrn.5364398

Abstract

The Inescapable Chain challenges foundational assumptions in philosophy and political theory by arguing that exploitation is intrinsic to human existence rather than an occasional deviation from it. Control, manipulation, and oppression are examined as extensions of intelligence and self-interest, while freedom is treated as a pacifying narrative that can sustain systems of domination. The manuscript argues that revolutions and reforms often reconfigure rather than abolish the architecture of oppression, and analyzes religion as an instrument of absolution and hierarchy. Drawing on philosophy, history, and psychology, it asks whether ethics and governance are ultimately negotiations of power rather than realizations of autonomy, and whether genuine rupture from recurring systems of control is possible.

Contribution

Connects philosophical, historical, and psychological perspectives in a broad analytical argument.

Method

Interdisciplinary argumentative synthesis.

Limits and availability

The claims are broad, the work is a preprint, and the local manifestation may differ from the SSRN version.
