Belief, Faith, and the Misclassification of Religious Commitment

Mohnish Shende · SSRN · 2026

Canonical record ↗DOI 10.2139/ssrn.6447879

Abstract

Debates concerning God are usually framed as disputes over the truth of a proposition and therefore over what to believe. This paper argues that such debates are often distorted by a prior classificatory error: belief, disbelief, suspension of judgment, acceptance, and faith are treated as instances of one doxastic type. That assimilation exports norms proper to belief into contexts where they do not fully apply, redistributing burdens of proof and causing criticisms to miss their targets. The paper distinguishes the attitude-types and their normative profiles, then uses worked examples to show how misclassification produces norm misapplication across epistemic, practical, and relational domains. Its aim is not to decide between theism and atheism, but to explain why intelligent participants can remain at an impasse while evaluating different stances under incompatible assumptions.

Contribution

Separates epistemic and practical attitudes to clarify how religious commitment is classified.

Method

Conceptual analysis with worked examples.

Limits and availability

This record is an SSRN preprint and has not been presented as peer reviewed.
